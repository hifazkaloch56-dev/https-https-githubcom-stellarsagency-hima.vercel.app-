CREDENTIALING & PROVIDER ENROLLMENT

CAQH Updates Every Provider Must Know in 2026

Published August 2026

Provider credentialing depends on accurate and up-to-date information. For many healthcare providers, the Council for Affordable Quality Healthcare (CAQH) profile remains an important part of the credentialing and payer enrollment process.

An incomplete, outdated, or inconsistent CAQH profile can create additional work during credentialing and may contribute to delays when health plans review provider information.

Key takeaway: Keeping your CAQH profile accurate should be treated as an ongoing credentialing responsibility, not a one-time setup task.

1. Keep Your CAQH Profile Up to Date

Provider information can change frequently. Addresses, phone numbers, employment details, practice locations, licenses, malpractice coverage, and other professional information may need to be updated when changes occur.

Providers should regularly review their CAQH profile and make sure the information matches the records being submitted to health plans.

Inconsistent information between CAQH, payer applications, NPPES, state licenses, and other credentialing records can create additional verification work.

  • Review practice and contact information.
  • Check current practice locations.
  • Confirm employment and affiliation information.
  • Review professional licenses and certifications.
  • Make sure supporting documents are current.

2. Do Not Treat CAQH Attestation as a One-Time Task

CAQH profiles require ongoing maintenance and re-attestation. Providers should pay attention to their CAQH account and complete required updates and attestations within the applicable timeframe.

Missing an attestation or allowing important information to become outdated can create unnecessary credentialing complications.

Best practice: Establish a recurring credentialing calendar so CAQH reviews, document updates, and other enrollment tasks are not forgotten.

3. Review Your Documents Before They Expire

Credentialing teams often need current documentation when reviewing provider information. Documents such as licenses, DEA certificates when applicable, malpractice insurance, certifications, and other credentials should be monitored for expiration dates.

Waiting until a document has already expired can make credentialing and payer enrollment more difficult.

A proactive document tracking process allows providers or credentialing teams to identify upcoming expirations and take action before they become a problem.

4. Make Sure CAQH Information Matches Payer Records

Updating CAQH is only one part of maintaining accurate provider data. Healthcare practices should also consider whether the information on file with individual payers is accurate.

Differences in practice addresses, taxonomy, provider affiliations, contact information, specialties, or other credentialing data may require additional review.

A regular comparison of CAQH information with payer records can help practices identify discrepancies before they become larger administrative issues.

5. Monitor Practice Locations and Provider Affiliations

Providers may work at multiple locations or have relationships with different healthcare organizations. These changes should be reflected accurately in credentialing records where applicable.

Practices should review provider affiliations and locations whenever a provider joins a new organization, leaves a practice, changes locations, or adds a new service location.

Keeping these details organized can make payer enrollment and credentialing maintenance more efficient.

6. Check for Incomplete or Inconsistent Information

Small inconsistencies can create unnecessary questions during credentialing. Providers should review their profiles carefully rather than assuming that previously submitted information is still correct.

Pay particular attention to names, addresses, specialty information, licenses, education, employment history, and supporting documentation.

When information changes, update the appropriate records and retain documentation that supports the change.

7. Create a Credentialing Maintenance Process

One of the most effective ways to manage CAQH is to make credentialing maintenance part of the practice's regular administrative workflow.

Instead of reviewing profiles only when a payer requests an update, practices can use a centralized tracking system to monitor provider information and important credentialing dates.

  • Maintain a provider credentialing checklist.
  • Track license and certification expiration dates.
  • Monitor CAQH attestation requirements.
  • Review provider information regularly.
  • Keep payer enrollment records organized.

Why CAQH Maintenance Matters for Practices

Credentialing delays can affect more than administrative workload. When provider information is not properly maintained, practices may spend additional time resolving enrollment questions, updating records, or communicating with health plans.

A proactive credentialing process helps practices keep provider information organized and reduces the risk of avoidable administrative delays.

Remember: CAQH maintenance should be part of an ongoing credentialing strategy. Regular reviews are easier and more efficient than trying to correct multiple outdated records at once.

Conclusion

CAQH remains an important part of the credentialing process for many healthcare providers. In 2026, practices should continue treating provider data accuracy, document management, and profile maintenance as ongoing responsibilities.

By keeping CAQH information current, monitoring documents, reviewing provider affiliations, and maintaining organized credentialing workflows, healthcare organizations can make payer enrollment and credentialing maintenance more efficient.
