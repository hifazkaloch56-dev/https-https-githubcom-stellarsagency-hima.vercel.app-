ICD-10 Coding Pitfalls: Avoiding Compliance Risk

For Healthcare CEOs & Practice Managers | Updated Aug 2026

For a CEO or Practice Manager, coding isn’t just a back-office task. It’s where revenue starts and where compliance risk lives.

One wrong ICD-10 code can do 3 things: delay payment, trigger an audit, and damage your reputation with payers. And in 2026, with AI audits and payer scrutiny at an all-time high, "small mistakes" are getting expensive fast.

Here are the 5 biggest ICD-10 coding pitfalls I see hurting practices — and how to fix them before they cost you.

1. Using "Unspecified" Codes as a Default

The fastest way to code is also the riskiest. R10.9 - Abdominal pain, unspecified is easy to drop in. But payers are denying these claims at a higher rate than ever.

Why it’s a problem: Unspecified codes tell the payer you don’t have enough documentation to prove medical necessity. For Medicare and commercial payers, that’s a red flag.

CEO Fix: Train providers to document specificity: laterality, acuity, cause, and site. M54.5 - Low back pain is weak. M54.51 - Vertebrogenic low back pain is defensible. Hold weekly chart reviews. Reward coders and providers who reduce unspecified usage by 20%.

2. Coding from the Diagnosis List Instead of the Note

EMRs make it too easy. The provider checks a box for "Diabetes" and the coder bills E11.9. But if the note doesn’t support it that day, you’ve got a compliance issue.

Why it’s a problem: This is the #1 reason for audit takebacks. Payers match the claim to the note. If it’s not there, they assume upcoding.

CEO Fix: Enforce "code what’s documented, not what’s on the problem list." Build a 2-step QA: Coder codes, then a lead coder audits 10 charts per provider per month. It takes 30 minutes and saves hundreds of thousands in takebacks.

3. Missing HCC and Risk Adjustment Codes

If you’re in Medicare Advantage, ACOs, or value-based contracts, this one hits your bottom line directly. Coders focus on the primary diagnosis for the visit and forget chronic conditions.

Why it’s a problem: If you don’t code a patient’s COPD, CKD, or CHF every year, the payer assumes the patient got healthier. Your RAF score drops. Your capitation drops.

CEO Fix: Make HCC capture a KPI. Run a gap report monthly. Ask providers: "Which of this patient’s 5 chronic conditions did we address and document today?" One extra HCC code per MA patient per year can mean $3,000+ in annual revenue.

4. Not Keeping Up with ICD-10 Annual Updates

ICD-10 updates every October 1st. New codes are added. Old codes are deleted. Guidelines change. Most practices update the system but never train the team.

Why it’s a problem: Using a deleted code = instant denial. Using a new code wrong = audit risk.

CEO Fix: Put "ICD-10 Update Training" on the calendar for September every year. 1 hour. Cover the top 10 changes relevant to your specialty. Your RCM vendor or clearinghouse usually provides this for free. Use it.

5. Poor Communication Between Providers and Coders

This is the root cause of 80% of errors. The provider writes "follow up DM". The coder has to guess: Type 1? Type 2? With complications? Without?

Why it’s a problem: Guesswork creates denials and compliance exposure. Auditors don’t accept "the coder assumed".

CEO Fix: Build a feedback loop. When a claim is denied for coding, the coder should send 1-sentence feedback to the provider within 48 hours. No blame. Just: "For diabetes, we need type and any complications documented." Over 90 days, denials drop dramatically.

The Bottom Line for Leaders

You don’t need to become a certified coder. But you do need to treat coding as a revenue and risk function, not admin work.

Here’s a 3-point checklist to run this quarter:

  • Audit: Pull your top 10 most-used ICD-10 codes. How many are "unspecified"?
  • Train: 1 hour on documentation specificity for providers. 1 hour on annual updates for coders.
  • Measure: Track 2 metrics monthly: Denial rate due to coding, and % of unspecified codes.

Clean coding protects you from audits, speeds up cash, and makes your value-based contracts more profitable.

In healthcare, compliance isn’t the cost of doing business. It’s the cost of doing business wrong. Get ahead of these pitfalls now, and your revenue cycle will thank you later.
